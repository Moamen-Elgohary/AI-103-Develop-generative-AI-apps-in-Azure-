# Wedding and Event Flower Planning

Logistics for planning florals for weddings and large events — timelines, quantities, and roles of different pieces. This covers planning and structure; for the meaning behind specific flower/color choices, see flower symbolism guidance.

## Order timelines

Florals should generally be booked 4–6 months ahead for weddings, and earlier (6–9 months) for peak wedding season (late spring through early fall) or if any tightly seasonal flowers (peonies, garden roses, dahlias, lilac) are wanted. Final flower counts and design details are typically confirmed with the florist 2–3 weeks before the event, once the final guest count and venue layout are settled. For corporate events or smaller occasions, 2–4 weeks' notice is usually sufficient unless a specific rare or heavily seasonal flower is required.

## Estimating quantities by event size

A rough planning guideline: small weddings (under 50 guests) typically need 8–12 centerpieces, one bridal bouquet, 4–6 bridesmaid bouquets, and 4–8 boutonnieres/corsages. Medium weddings (50–150 guests) scale to 12–20 centerpieces and proportionally more personal flowers. Large weddings (150+ guests) often need 20+ centerpieces and may split budget between full arrangements for key tables and simpler bud vases for others to manage cost. For non-wedding events (galas, corporate functions), centerpiece count is usually driven by table count rather than guest count — one arrangement per table is standard.

## Ceremony vs. reception flowers

Ceremony flowers (aisle arrangements, arch/arbor florals, altar pieces) are typically designed to be repurposed for the reception where possible — arch flowers moved to flank the head table, aisle arrangements reused as entrance décor — to reduce total flower spend. Reception flowers (centerpieces, escort card table, cake table) tend to make up the largest share of a wedding's floral budget since they're needed in the highest volume and are on display longest. Ceremony flowers are viewed for a shorter window and are sometimes designed slightly less densely as a result.

## Personal flowers: bouquets, boutonnieres, and corsages

The bridal bouquet is usually the most elaborate personal piece and often sets the color/style palette for the rest of the wedding's florals. Bridesmaid bouquets are typically smaller and simpler versions of the bridal bouquet, sometimes in a complementary rather than identical palette. Boutonnieres (worn by groomsmen, fathers, ushers) are single-flower or small clustered pieces pinned to a lapel — sturdy, low-maintenance flowers (spray roses, succulents, small orchids) hold up best over a long event. Corsages (worn by mothers, grandmothers) are traditionally wrist-worn or pinned and use similarly durable flowers, coordinated with the wedding's palette without necessarily matching it exactly.

## Budget allocation

A common rule of thumb allocates the wedding floral budget roughly as: 40% reception centerpieces and décor, 25% ceremony florals, 20% personal flowers (bouquets, boutonnieres, corsages), and 15% miscellaneous (delivery, setup, floral preservation, extras). Couples working with a tighter budget often reduce centerpiece count or complexity first, since it's the largest line item, while keeping the bridal bouquet and ceremony backdrop as priorities since they appear most in photos.
